## Researcher Reported Missing After Cryptic Transmission

_Ceres Standard Newsfeed – Security Correspondent L. Marquez_

Ceres Station’s orbital arrays registered a brief, distorted transmission at 0327 hours three nights ago. The signal lasted less than twenty seconds but carried the encrypted tag of a registered biotech researcher, Dr. Lira Vester. Hours later, she was gone.

The message—its final fragment captured by civilian traffic control—contained one intelligible phrase: “It’s learning.” The rest was static.

Security footage shows Vester entering Dock G-17 with a single case and boarding an unregistered freighter listed as the _Lunette_. That ship departed ten minutes later on a vector toward the outer Belt and has not filed a flight path since.

Protogen Biotech, Vester’s employer, released a statement describing her as “on medical leave.” When questioned about the transmission, the company denied any ongoing experiments involving artificial neural mapping, though records show Vester’s team specialized in cortical lattice simulations—research that was quietly suspended months ago by UN regulatory order.

A former colleague, speaking under condition of anonymity, claims Vester had grown increasingly paranoid in recent weeks. “She said something had followed her from a test node,” the colleague said. “That the data was rewriting itself.”

OPA authorities have issued no warrant, citing lack of jurisdiction. UN investigators briefly considered the case a routine disappearance until an independent comms analyst traced a secondary echo of Vester’s message bouncing through a relay near Hygiea—on a military frequency long thought decommissioned.

Since then, speculation has spiralled. Some Belt news feeds suggest she fled to expose corporate malfeasance. Others hint at dangerous research connected to past Protogen projects on neural adaptation. Conspiracy groups on Medina’s networks have already dubbed her “The Ghost Coder.”

A veteran system-net technician offered a blunter theory: “You don’t send something like that unless you’ve seen it do something it shouldn’t.”

The UNN’s Office of Science Oversight has dispatched an inquiry but cautioned against “panic fuelled by fiction.” Yet the office refused to confirm whether similar incidents have occurred elsewhere.

For now, Dr. Lira Vester’s signal has joined the constant hum of Belt traffic—another echo fading into the static. Whether it was a warning, confession, or hallucination remains unknown. But in the labs she left behind, her terminal still runs a process that no one can open or stop.

## Freighter Loss Near the Belt Raises Questions

_Ganymede Observer – Special Investigative Report by Alim Sayegh_

The wreckage of the _Aldous Huxley_, a mid-tonnage freighter registered out of Tycho, was found drifting in a slow spin near Pallas orbit last week. Its distress beacon never activated. What the UNN salvage crew found inside has unsettled even veteran responders.

Every crew member—twelve in total—is missing. The ship’s compartments show signs of rapid decompression and melted bulkheads, but the flight log indicates no reactor breach or weapon impact. “It’s like something ate the air and left the walls half-alive,” one rescuer said over encrypted comms.

The _Huxley_’s cargo manifest listed gene-repair cultures and cryogenic medical stock bound for a research facility on Callisto. Yet investigators recovered dozens of sealed canisters unregistered to any legal manifest. When opened, each contained a viscous grey film later classified as “bio-reactive substrate.” The material dissolved on contact with atmospheric oxygen.

UNN spokesperson Commander Karun D’Silva refused to comment on those findings, calling them “speculative Belt gossip.” Still, leaked inspection notes mention trace neural compounds similar to experimental memory gels once patented by Protogen.

Survivors’ families are demanding answers. “My brother flew clean his whole life,” said Yomi Khanna, sister of the ship’s engineer. “Now they tell me they found residue, not remains. What does that even mean?”

Independent scientists consulted by the _Observer_ caution against sensationalism but admit confusion. “Organic contamination can occur through improper sealing,” said Dr. Mehta of the University of Luna. “But synchronized system failure across multiple decks suggests interference—mechanical or biological.”

Belter rumour feeds have their own explanations: a failed corporate weapons test, a rogue AI, even remnants of “the blue ghost”—the protomolecule stories that never quite died.

Martian patrols have increased sweeps along nearby lanes, citing “pirate activity.” Yet no signs of boarding or looting were recorded on the _Huxley_. Its cargo holds were intact—except for one section of scorched plating that appears to have melted from the inside out.

The _Huxley_ now orbits Pallas in a controlled drift, quarantined and sealed. UNN advisories warn independent salvagers to stay clear. “Potential biohazard,” the beacon loops in seven languages. But for Belters passing by, it has already earned another name whispered on open channels: _Ghost Freighter_.

## Tycho Station Reports Record Tonnage of Manufactured Components Shipped in Q3

_By BHIS Industrial Desk_

TYCHO STATION — Tycho Manufacturing and Engineering announced its highest single-quarter output since the station's founding, with 340,000 tonnes of fabricated components shipped across 19 Belt destinations in the third quarter. The figure represents a 31% increase over the same period last year.

The bulk of the output consisted of modular habitat sections, pressure door assemblies, and water reclamation units — all categories of equipment in acute demand across the Belt following the cascading supply disruptions that began after Eros.

Fred Johnson's office issued a brief statement marking the milestone, calling it evidence of the Belt's capacity for self-reliance.

Observers have noted that several of the listed destination stations are associated with OPA-aligned infrastructure projects. Two destinations — listed as "contracted private recipients" — were not named in the public figures.

Masstech analysts monitoring Tycho's procurement contracts reported that raw material orders have outpaced stated production capacity, suggesting either stockpiling or output beyond what has been disclosed.

Tycho did not respond to a request for clarification.

### Freighter Insurance Costs Climb in Outer System  
Shipping insurers have raised premiums for freighters operating beyond the Jovian system following several recent piracy incidents in sparsely patrolled regions of space. While most attacks remain small-scale cargo thefts, shipping companies report a growing trend of coordinated operations targeting isolated transport vessels. Industry representatives have urged governments and private security firms to expand patrol coverage, though Belter organisations warn that increased militarisation could further destabilise fragile frontier communities.

### Tycho Free News Journalist Reported Missing en Route to Mathilde Station
Tycho Free News has reported that correspondent Priya Sethuram has been out of contact for nine days. Sethuram was travelling to Mathilde Station to follow up on the work stoppage story published last month. Her ship, a chartered Osprey-class courier, is not showing on any registered transit log. Tycho Free News has formally requested information from Kruger-Thompson, Pinkwater Security, and the Ceres Port Authority. No response has been received from any party.

### Earth General Assembly Passes Resolution Condemning OPA "Paramilitary Expansion"
The UN General Assembly passed a non-binding resolution by a margin of 61 to 34 calling on the OPA to "cease the development and deployment of paramilitary forces in civilian space." The resolution was introduced by the Earth bloc following reports of armed OPA vessels near Ceres. The OPA is not a recognised signatory to any UN treaty framework and is under no legal obligation to respond. Anderson Dawes called the resolution "noise from people who have never been to the Belt."

### Voltaire Collective Claims Responsibility for Ransacked Pinkwater Depot on Ilus Station
An encrypted message circulated through OPA-adjacent feeds this week claimed the Voltaire Collective had raided a Pinkwater Security equipment depot on Ilus Station, destroying two armoured personnel carriers and taking medical supplies for redistribution. Pinkwater has not confirmed the incident. Ilus Station authority has not responded to enquiries.
