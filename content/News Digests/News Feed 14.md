## Tycho Station Announces Mission to Intercept the LDSS Nauvoo Before It Leaves the System
_By Dalvi Okeke, Senior Correspondent – Tycho Free News_

__TYCHO STATION__— In a move hailed as both practical and symbolic, Tycho Station has confirmed preparations for Project Reclaim—a mission to intercept and recover the LDSS Nauvoo before the massive generational vessel leaves the solar system entirely.

The Nauvoo, launched just over a year ago in an emergency bid to redirect Eros Station, has since drifted far beyond the Belt and is now on an unpowered trajectory toward permanent interstellar escape. With calculations showing the ship will cross the heliopause within fourteen months, Tycho’s leadership argues that it is now—or never.

### Fred Johnson: “The Belt Builds. The Belt Claims.”
At a press briefing earlier today, Tycho’s Chief Operations Executive Fred Johnson laid out the reasoning behind the mission:

_"The Nauvoo was built by Belters, in Belter space, by Belter hands. We gave it up when the system needed it. And now it drifts, abandoned. The Belt does not abandon its own. We will bring it home."_

Johnson emphasized that while the vessel was originally constructed under contract with the Church of Latter-Day Saints, its use in the Eros incident and subsequent abandonment render its legal status "in flux."

_"The High Court made it clear—it wasn’t theft. There was no handover, but there was no crime either. So the legal ownership is unresolved. But moral ownership? That belongs to us."_

### Legal Limbo and Salvage Ethics
Indeed, a recent Luna High Court ruling determined that the repurposing of the Nauvoo did not constitute theft, as no formal transfer of ownership had taken place at the time of launch. The court refrained from deciding the question of rightful ownership, sending the case back to lower interplanetary arbitration for a contractual review.

While the LDS Church has reasserted its claim, it has taken no public steps toward recovering the vessel. This vacuum of action has emboldened Tycho to proceed, with the OPA broadly backing the initiative.

_"We’re not stealing a ship. We’re recovering a legacy,"_ Johnson added.

### Mission Logistics: A Race Against Escape Velocity
Project Reclaim will involve a convoy of deep-range salvage tugs, pre-rigged with long-burn drive modules and self-sustaining drone packages. The mission aims to rendezvous with the Nauvoo within six months, conduct structural integrity checks, and, if possible, reactivate onboard systems to assist in slowing or redirecting the ship’s trajectory.

If no course correction is applied, the Nauvoo is projected to pass the heliosphere boundary within the next year, after which recovery becomes exponentially more difficult.

### Visions for the Future
Tycho officials have not disclosed final plans for the vessel’s use if recovered, but internal proposals include:

- A mobile Belt logistics platform
    
- A deep-space scientific outpost
    
- A permanent Belt governance seat, independent of Ceres or Tycho politics
    

Some within the OPA have even suggested the Nauvoo be renamed and recommissioned as the first Belter interstellar ark, a symbol of unity and ambition beyond the Inners’ reach.

_"They called it a ship of faith,"_ Johnson said. _"Let it be that again—but this time, faith in ourselves."_

Project Reclaim’s first deployment is scheduled within ninety days.

## Leak Suggests High-Level Meeting Between Fred Johnson and Anderson Dawes on Belt Policing

_By Saba Rojas, Tycho Independent_

A confidential source has revealed that Fred Johnson and Anderson Dawes held a closed-door meeting last week to discuss coordinated policing of the asteroid belt’s primary trade lanes.

According to the leak, the discussion focused on reducing piracy, stabilising shipping insurance rates, and presenting a unified OPA-backed enforcement presence in high-traffic corridors between Ceres, Tycho, and the Jovian routes. The source described the talks as “direct, pragmatic, and overdue,” though no formal communiqué has been issued.

Independent captains have reported a noticeable increase in informal inspections by ships flying various OPA-aligned colours. Some see this as a sign of improved organisation; others worry it signals the consolidation of power by larger factions at the expense of smaller cells.

When approached for comment, Johnson declined to confirm or deny the existence of any agreement, stating only that “the Belt deserves safe passage for its own ships.” Dawes’ office issued a brief statement saying he would not discuss “private matters between Belters.”

Neither man would address whether any revenue-sharing arrangements or enforcement standards were formalised. Analysts suggest that even tacit coordination between their respective power bases could significantly reshape control of the Belt’s trade arteries.

For now, any agreement remains unofficial. What is clear is that Belt trade lanes—long contested by pirates, corporations, and rival factions—may soon operate under a more unified, if opaque, authority.

## Rumours Swirl Around Decommissioned Research Platforms

_By Arun Vale, Outer System Observer_

Unverified reports circulating among Belt pilots suggest that several decommissioned research platforms near the Saturnian system have been quietly transferred to private ownership.

Neither Earth nor Mars has acknowledged the transactions. Shipping registries show irregular gaps in ownership records, though such discrepancies are not uncommon in the outer system.

Analysts caution against speculation, noting that ageing platforms frequently change hands and are often stripped for parts.

In a climate of rising tension and corporate secrecy, even mundane transfers can feed persistent rumours.
