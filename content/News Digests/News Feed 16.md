## Pinkwater Expands Security Contract on Ganymede

_By Haruto Iqbal, Jovian Trade Network_

Pinkwater Security has secured an expanded public safety contract in the agricultural sectors of Ganymede, citing increased smuggling and labour unrest.

Corporate representatives describe the move as preventative. “Food production on Ganymede is critical to the entire system,” said a company liaison. “We are ensuring that external agitators do not compromise vital infrastructure.”

Belter labour delegates see it differently. Several hydroponics technicians report more frequent ID checks and unannounced dormitory inspections. Shipping records show a steady increase in Pinkwater personnel and materiel arriving over the last two months, including heavier armour and upgraded sensor arrays.

Critics argue that the militarisation of what is ostensibly a civilian breadbasket risks escalating tensions. Increased security presence may stabilise yields in the short term, but it also underscores how dependent the Belt remains on Inner capital and enforcement.

## Martian Naval Exercises Near Callisto Raise Eyebrows

_By Leena Okonkwo, The Martian Herald_

The Martian Congressional Republic Navy has confirmed large-scale manoeuvres in the Jovian system, centred on the orbit of Callisto. Official statements describe the exercise as a scheduled readiness drill.

Multiple capital-class vessels and support craft have been detected conducting coordinated burns and simulated electronic warfare operations. Traffic advisories were issued to civilian vessels, temporarily restricting approach corridors.

Earth’s UNN has responded cautiously, describing the drills as within Martian sovereign rights. Nonetheless, diplomatic channels between Earth and Mars are reportedly active, with both sides keen to avoid misinterpretation.

For Belters operating in the Jovian lanes, the message is clear: traffic will be slower, inspections more frequent, and tolerance for deviations slimmer.

## OPA Factions Clash Over Shipping Levies in the Belt

_By Saba Rojas, Tycho Independent_

Rival factions within the Outer Planets Alliance are disputing the imposition of new security levies on independent haulers transiting certain Belt corridors.

Supporters argue the fees are necessary to fund patrol ships and protect against piracy. Opponents counter that the levies resemble the very Inner exploitation the OPA was formed to resist.

On Tycho Station, heated assemblies have drawn large crowds. Speakers denounced both Earth and Mars, while also criticising OPA leaders for perceived overreach. Several shipping guilds have temporarily rerouted vessels to avoid contested sectors.

The dispute highlights a broader tension within the Belt: balancing self-governance with economic survival.

## Other News
### Ceres Implements Basic Income Pilot for Belters  
Under Dawes’ leadership, Ceres has launched a pilot program offering a small monthly stipend to registered station citizens. Funded by new tariffs on Inner cargo routes, the program aims to stabilize civil unrest—but critics say it’s unsustainable without further OPA centralization.

### UN Reviews Expansion of Private Security Contracts  
United Nations transit authorities confirmed they are reviewing several new proposals to expand private security coverage at remote refuelling stations and cargo hubs across the Belt. The move follows rising piracy reports and growing insurance costs for shipping lanes beyond Mars. Critics warn that expanding corporate security presence risks repeating the controversies surrounding firms such as Star Helix and Pinkwater Security, whose operations have often drawn accusations of corruption, excessive force, and weak accountability to local populations.

### Luxury Tourism to Titan Continues to Rise  
Passenger numbers travelling to the domed resorts of Titan have increased sharply this quarter, according to figures released by Saturnalia Cruises and other Inner-system travel operators. The boom in high-end tourism has sparked a surge in support services along the Saturn transit corridor, including refuelling outposts and private dockyards catering exclusively to luxury vessels. Belt residents have criticised the expansion, noting that the infrastructure supporting wealthy tourists rarely improves conditions for the Belter workers who maintain it.
