## Dharma Sandesh: A Call to Reflection and Righteous Action  
*In Response to the Eros Catastrophe*  
*Issued by the Akhil Surya Dharma Parishad (Universal Solar Council of Dharma)*  
*Date: Shravana Purnima, Vikram Samvat 2409 | August 12, 2352 CE*  
*Location: Vedic Assembly Hall, Arya Sabha Mandir, Patanjal Station, Low Earth Orbit*


> ॐ असतो मा सद्गमय। तमसो मा ज्योतिर्गमय। मृत्योर्मा अमृतं गमय।  
> “Lead us from the unreal to the real, from darkness to light, from death to immortality.” — Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 1.3.28
> 
---

### Preamble
Six months have passed since the great sorrow that fell upon Eros Station. The cries of the dead still echo in the corridors of our minds. What transpired was a profound violation of ṛta—the cosmic order.

As the Akhil Surya Dharma Parishad—comprising spiritual leaders and teachers from Sanātana Dharma traditions across Earth, Mars, Luna, and the Belt—we offer this Dharma Sandesh (message of righteousness) to guide seekers and householders alike.

### I. The Moral Nature of the Eros Incident
The manipulation of life through the substance called the “protomolecule” was an act of adharma—an abandonment of moral responsibility, wisdom (viveka), and compassion (karuṇā). The slaughter of innocents for technological or political gain is the lowest expression of tamasic action—one rooted in delusion and destruction.

As Lord Krishna teaches in the Bhagavad Gītā (3.6):  
“Actions performed without understanding, in delusion and without concern for consequences, are tāmasika karma, and they bind the soul to sorrow.”

Let there be no confusion: Eros was not fate, nor the will of the gods—it was the result of human ignorance and moral decay.

### II. The Sacredness of All Beings
In Sanātana Dharma, all beings—regardless of birth, caste, planet, or station—are manifestations of the same Divine Consciousness (Brahman). The victims of Eros were not mere casualties; they were ātmans, each carrying the spark of the infinite.

We call upon all dharmic communities to:
- Offer śrāddha (ancestral rites) for the unclaimed souls of Eros.
- Chant the Mṛtyuñjaya Mantra during full moons to honor the dead and ease their passage.
- Establish Ekaṭṭa Smāraka Mandirs (Unity Memorial Shrines) in major habitats to remember the fallen as universal kin.

### III. On the Protomolecule and the Unknown
What we call the protomolecule is part of the great web of prakṛti—nature. It is neither inherently good nor evil. Like fire, it may warm or burn; like water, it may nourish or drown.

But to approach such forces without humility (vinaya), and to weaponize them without wisdom (jñāna), is to invite imbalance. True knowledge (vidyā) is that which uplifts all beings, not that which dominates them.

Let our scientists be ṛṣis of the modern age—seeking truth, guided by dharma, not by profit or power.

### IV. Dharma in the Belt and Outer Worlds
We remind all Hindus that dharma is not geography-bound. Whether on Earth or Eros, Luna or Tycho, a person of dharma must:
- Offer service (sevā) where there is suffering
- Share wisdom where there is confusion
- Protect life where it is endangered

We call for the expansion of Dharma Seva Kendras (Centers of Service and Wisdom) across the Belt and outer colonies, to bring food, counsel, medical aid, and spiritual guidance to the marginalized.

The time has come for a Dharmic Mission to the Belt, led not with conversion, but with compassion.

### V. Unity of Traditions in Times of Trial

In this age of dislocation and distrust, let Hindus not fall into division over caste, sect, or regional identity. Let us embrace the teaching of the Rig Veda:

> “Ekam sat, viprā bahudhā vadanti”  
> “Truth is One; the wise speak of it in many ways.”

Let us also reach out to our sisters and brothers of all faiths. The sorrow of Eros is not Hindu sorrow—it is human sorrow. As vasudhaiva kuṭumbakam teaches us: “The world is one family.”

### Closing Benediction
Let us walk together in the path of light, in remembrance of those lost, and in responsibility for the world we now shape.

Let our actions be rooted in ahimsā, our speech in satya, and our vision in dharma.

May peace be upon the fallen. May wisdom guide the living. May balance be restored.

Om. Peace, peace, peace.
