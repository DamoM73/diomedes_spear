The new contract forced upon the Belter workers by Kruger-Thompson Mining is a blatant attempt to reduce costs, increase control, and strip away workers’ rights. It is exploitative, dangerous, and nearly impossible to refuse for those who rely on the job to survive. Below are ten of the worst clauses:

## Key concerns
### 1. Indefinite Contract Duration ("Service Until Completion")
- Workers are contractually bound until their assigned mining operation is deemed “complete”—a vague and open-ended condition determined only by company officials.
- Anyone who attempts to leave before completion is considered in breach of contract and forfeits all wages.

### 2. Pay Deductions for "Operational Costs"
- Workers' pay is subject to company-determined deductions for housing, air, water, equipment maintenance, and even transit fees between work shifts.
- Many end up owing more than they earn, keeping them trapped in a cycle of corporate debt.

### 3. Mandatory "Productivity Quotas" with No Upper Limit
- Each worker has a daily tonnage quota, which increases every quarter without additional pay.
- Failure to meet quotas results in pay deductions or disciplinary action, including loss of rations or air reduction penalties.

### 4. "Workplace Safety Waiver" – No Liability for Injury or Death
- The company takes no responsibility for accidents, radiation exposure, equipment failures, or medical issues caused by working conditions.
- Any worker injured on the job must either pay for their own medical expenses (often more than their total earnings) or work through the injury.

### 5. No Right to Organize or Strike

- Any attempt to form a union, protest, or collectively bargain is considered a breach of contract and can lead to termination without pay or even blacklisting across other Belt stations.
- Workers suspected of organizing resistance may be relocated or reassigned to hazardous duties without warning.

### 6. Punitive Air and Water Rationing
- The company has full control over air and water distribution, which can be reduced as a form of punishment for perceived insubordination, slow work, or complaints.
- Dissenters can be placed on “low-priority” supply lists, meaning they suffer shortages while others continue to receive normal rations.

### 7. Forced Overtime Without Additional Pay
- Management has the right to extend shifts up to 16 hours per cycle, overriding personal time and rest periods.
- Workers cannot refuse overtime without financial penalties or job reassignment to the most dangerous sections of the mine.

### 8. Immediate Termination for Medical Inability
- Any worker who is injured, sick, or physically unable to work for more than five cycles is considered "unfit for duty" and terminated without severance.
- The company provides no return transport for terminated workers, leaving them stranded on Mathilde Station unless they can pay their own way off-station.

### 9. Restricted Communication and Surveillance
- All worker communications are monitored, and any unauthorized contact with outsiders (including journalists or OPA factions) is grounds for immediate termination.
- Workers must submit to regular surveillance checks, and private quarters are subject to random searches.

### 10. No Exit Without Paying "Departure Fees"
- Workers who wish to leave Mathilde Station before their contract expires must pay a departure fee equivalent to six months of wages to cover “losses incurred by the company”.
- Those unable to pay are either forced to stay or blacklisted across corporate stations, making it impossible to find future work.
