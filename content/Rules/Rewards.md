## Income
Most adventures should increase the characters’ Income scores by +1. GMs may opt to boost the Income increase to +2 in the case of significant windfalls or provide temporary Income.

### Temporary Income
GMs may opt to grant a temporary bonus that represents a limited windfall:
- character gains a +1 to +3 bonus to the next Income test
- If the test succeeds, the bonus is used up and gone
- character has the option of dividing up a temporary Income bonus among different Income tests but, once the test succeeds, that portion of the bonus is gone
- any depletion of the character’s Income comes off the temporary bonus first and lost bonus points cannot be recovered

## Reputation
Characters can leverage their reputation to smooth social interactions or get access to things out of the reach of other people.

### Honorifics
#### Personal Reputation
Characters can invoke their reputation to gain an advantage in a conflict:
- opposed test involving the character’s famed skill or natural ability -> opponents suffer a -1 penalty to their opposing roll.

#### Supportive Reputation
Friends or foes can invoke their honorific in support of their actions:
- requires someone other than the character to talk up their title or tell convincing stories of the deeds required to earn it to someone outside of the character’s party
- the character with the reputation being supported gains a +2 bonus to any roll in which they demonstrate the abilities they’re known for

#### Inspired Reputation
A character using their honorific outside of their field of interest or expertise is calling on it for an inspired purpose. Inspired use of reputation grants a character a +1 bonus to the result of the Drama Die when the player rolls it to achieve an aim in which they demonstrate smart use of the honorific.

#### Passive Reputation
Characters generally make passive use of their honorifics more often than active use. When dealing with a group or individual aware of a character’s reputation and likely to be affected by it directly, GMs should colour the interaction appropriately. 

In social situations, characters benefit from a free single step attitude shift in the direction suited to their reputation. This step isn’t always positive, as some people will dislike or mistrust a given honorific.

## Membership
Characters can obtain membership in organizations based on their backgrounds or professions, but their influence within those organizations depends upon their rank.

### Rank 1: Recruit
Characters newly inducted into an organization’s ranks receive a +1 bonus to all rolls related to positive dealings within the organisation. This bonus builds upon any prior good relations with the group, rising to a total +2 bonus for such characters.

Characters may access the group’s basic resources
- provided services
- shared credit accounts
- company housing
- political groups may offer access to related people in positions of power
- professional groups may provide certain services at a discount or even for free
- security and military organizations provide many of their members’ needs, as well as granting them access to equipment and weapons not readily available to civilians

### Rank 2: Respected Member
When they have finally reached this level, characters gain:
- +2 bonus to any tests involving other members of their organization at their rank or lower
- respected members also gain a +1 bonus in social interactions with non-members who have reason to respect or fear the organization
- access to their group’s full organizational resources, usually including other personnel
	- may command subordinates to perform easy encounters on their behalf
	- use organization resources to pay recruits to handle encounters of average difficulty
- access to all of their group’s resources, within reason:
	- wealth
	- equipment
	- information
	- access to high-ranking people with connections to the organization

At this rank, most groups expect characters to act in the group’s best interests, and not to waste resources on their own goals. Should a character abuse these resources, the leaders of the group may step in.

### Rank 3: Authority Figure
The true height of power within an organization, Rank 3 can be difficult to reach unless a character started the group them selves. 

Members of this rank wield tremendous power over other members of the organization:
- setting policy
- controlling access to the group’s resources and personnel
- +1 bonus in social situations where their control of the group would impress non members, in addition to the social bonus from Rank 2, for a total of +2.

## Relationships
Relationships should ideally form during play, arising from how a character treats another individual over time.

These relationships are measured by their Bond and Intensity:
- Bond is the nature of the relationship between the characters, such as friendship, hatred, or romance
- Intensity is the measure of that relationship’s strength

### Relationship Bond
Examples of relationship bonds:
- I would risk my life for you
- I won't stop until you're dead
- We've gone through hell together
- I trust you to be my conscience
- You are someone I love and respect, but we can never be together in that way I want

### Relationship Intensity
Relationship Intensity is measured with a rating of 1 to 5, with 1 being a new but significant relationship, and 5 being the kind of Bond characters experience only once in their lifetime.

Each rank of Intensity -> a character gets a free SP:
- must be spent on stunts relating to the relationship when the character’s actions are directly motivated by the relationship
- can only be spent once per session
- can be used on any test, not just when the player rolls doubles
- can be combined with SP generated by rolling doubles
- Intensity stunt points are available at the start of each game session, though GMs can rule that they refresh after a particularly cathartic interlude.
